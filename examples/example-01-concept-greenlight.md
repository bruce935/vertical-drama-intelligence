# Example 01 — Concept Greenlight

Version: 0.4

Purpose:

Demonstrate how Vertical Drama Intelligence evaluates a minimal vertical-drama concept before major development work begins.

This example focuses on:

- concept qualification,
- protagonist desire,
- conflict,
- stakes,
- serial engine,
- escalation capacity,
- reveal architecture,
- story obligations,
- development risks,
- and Greenlight decision.

This example does not use live market data.

It must not invent:

- retention percentages,
- audience size,
- search volume,
- revenue,
- conversion rate,
- or guaranteed virality.

---

# 1. User Input

Creator input:

> I want to make a 50-episode vertical revenge drama about a young woman who discovers at her father's funeral that she was secretly removed from his will. The company was left to her adopted brother, who appears loyal but may have manipulated the will. She decides to enter the company under a false identity and take back what she believes belongs to her.

Requested output:

Evaluate whether this concept is ready for development.

# 3. Information Status

## CONFIRMED

Confirmed by creator input:

- The protagonist is a young woman.
- Her father has died.
- She learns at the funeral that she was removed from the will.
- The company was left to her adopted brother.
- She suspects manipulation.
- She plans to enter the company under a false identity.
- She intends to reclaim what she believes belongs to her.

## ASSUMED

For development analysis only:

- The protagonist previously expected to inherit or share control.
- The adopted brother has enough authority to create meaningful opposition.
- The false identity can plausibly survive beyond the first few episodes.
- The company is large enough to support multiple internal factions.

These assumptions must be validated during Series Bible development.

## PENDING

Still unresolved:

- Whether the will was actually manipulated.
- Whether the adopted brother is guilty.
- Whether another party caused the inheritance change.
- Why the father changed or appeared to change the will.
- Whether the protagonist legally deserves the company.
- What evidence exists.
- Whether the protagonist's belief matches external truth.
- Whether the adopted brother is antagonist, victim, or mixed-role character.

# 7. Serial Engine

The concept has a potentially strong serial engine because the protagonist's objective cannot be solved in one action.

The engine can repeatedly generate episodes through:

ACCESS

→ DISCOVERY

→ INTERPRETATION

→ RISK

→ COUNTERMOVE

→ NEW INFORMATION

→ ESCALATION

The false identity creates a useful recurring constraint:

The protagonist wants more access while simultaneously avoiding recognition.

The inheritance mystery creates a second engine:

Every piece of evidence can change:

- what she believes,
- what the audience believes,
- what another character knows,
- and which alliance is rational.

The corporate environment provides a third engine:

Each new level of access can expose:

- documents,
- executives,
- family secrets,
- financial decisions,
- old conflicts,
- and competing motives.

Assessment:

SERIAL ENGINE = PROMISING

Status:

INFERRED

Reason:

The concept contains multiple renewable conflict sources rather than relying on one reveal.

# 8. Escalation Capacity

Potential escalation ladder:

1. Protagonist discovers she was removed from the will.
2. She suspects the adopted brother.
3. She enters the company under false identity.
4. She discovers the adopted brother may not have controlled the will change.
5. Another executive appears connected.
6. Evidence suggests the father expected internal betrayal.
7. The protagonist's false identity becomes vulnerable.
8. The adopted brother begins investigating her.
9. A trusted ally is exposed as unreliable.
10. Evidence supporting the protagonist's original theory is weakened.
11. The protagonist must choose between revenge and truth.
12. A deeper ownership or family secret changes the meaning of the inheritance.

This is an example architecture.

It is:

RECOMMENDED

not:

CONFIRMED CANON.

# 9. Reveal Architecture Potential

The concept supports layered reveals.

Possible reveal categories:

## Identity Reveal

Who the protagonist really is.

## Motive Reveal

Why she entered the company.

## Will Reveal

Whether the will was altered.

## Responsibility Reveal

Who caused the inheritance outcome.

## Father Reveal

What the father actually intended.

## Alliance Reveal

Which characters are loyal, deceptive, or misinformed.

## Self-Reversal Reveal

The protagonist may discover that one of her strongest beliefs was wrong.

This last category is strategically valuable because it prevents the story from becoming:

"I was betrayed, and every episode proves I was right."

A stronger mystery allows:

BELIEF

to evolve separately from:

TRUTH.

# 10. Cliffhanger Potential

The concept supports short-form cliffhangers such as:

- a document contradicting a known claim,
- a character recognizing part of the protagonist's identity,
- a hidden signature,
- a surveillance image,
- a secret meeting,
- an unexpected beneficiary,
- a destroyed record,
- a contradictory witness statement,
- a corporate vote,
- a relationship reveal,
- evidence that weakens the protagonist's theory.

Cliffhangers should not all use the same structure.

Avoid repetitive ending pattern:

"Someone walks into the room and looks shocked."

The meaning and consequence of each cliffhanger should change story state.

# 12. Belief vs Truth Opportunity

The strongest version of this concept should preserve:

PROTAGONIST BELIEF

separately from

EXTERNAL TRUTH.

Example:

Belief:

"My adopted brother stole my inheritance."

Truth Status:

PENDING

This allows the story to generate:

- suspicion,
- false certainty,
- contradictory evidence,
- behavioral consequences,
- relationship damage,
- and later reversal.

Hard rule:

The protagonist's accusation must not automatically become canon merely because she believes it.

# 14. Evidence Architecture Opportunity

The mystery should rely on distinguishable evidence objects rather than only dialogue.

Potential evidence classes:

- will versions,
- signature records,
- board minutes,
- emails,
- security footage,
- legal filings,
- financial transfers,
- voice messages,
- witness statements,
- calendar records,
- archived company documents.

Important:

Evidence should not automatically equal interpretation.

Example:

OBSERVED:

INFERRED:

The meeting may relate to the inheritance change.

Not valid without support:

The meeting proves the adopted brother manipulated the will.

# 16. Development Risks

## Risk 1 — One-Note Revenge

If every episode only confirms that the adopted brother is evil, the mystery engine weakens.

Mitigation:

Allow evidence to complicate the protagonist's belief.

---

## Risk 2 — Weak False Identity Logic

If there is no convincing reason she can enter the company without being recognized, audience trust may weaken.

Mitigation:

Define:

- prior absence,
- changed appearance if relevant,
- limited recognition exposure,
- role access,
- document identity,
- who knows her face,
- who does not.

Do not rely on unexplained recognition failure.

---

## Risk 3 — Evidence Convenience

If every new clue appears exactly when needed, the story may feel mechanically constructed.

Mitigation:

Build an evidence map with:

- provenance,
- access constraints,
- dependencies,
- uncertainty,
- and consequences.

---

## Risk 4 — Repetitive Corporate Obstacles

Repeated meetings, firings, and confrontations may become structurally repetitive.

Mitigation:

Vary pressure source:

LEGAL

FAMILY

ROMANTIC

EVIDENTIARY

CORPORATE

IDENTITY

PHYSICAL

PUBLIC

---

## Risk 5 — Weak Protagonist Cost

If infiltration has no real cost, tension becomes artificial.

Mitigation:

Make each gain create a new vulnerability.

---

## Risk 6 — Premature Villain Certainty

If the story locks the adopted brother as villain too early, later mystery becomes cosmetic.

Mitigation:

Keep responsibility status:

PENDING

until evidence justifies stronger canon.

However, 50 episodes should not be approved solely from the one-line premise.

3. Primary mystery answer
4. Reveal ladder

6. Story obligation map

The Series Bible should answer privately:

1. Was the will actually changed?
2. Who caused the change?
3. Why?
4. Did the father consent?
5. What did the adopted brother know?
6. What does the protagonist incorrectly believe?
7. What evidence exists at story start?
8. Which evidence is hidden?
9. Which evidence is misleading?
10. Which claims are false?

# 22. Initial Canon Recommendation

At concept stage, lock only what is necessary.

Recommended:

## LOCKED

- father is dead,
- protagonist was removed from the apparent inheritance outcome,
- adopted brother currently controls or receives the company,
- protagonist enters under false identity,
- protagonist initially suspects wrongdoing.

## PROPOSED

- adopted brother directly manipulated the will,
- third-party conspiracy,
- father intentionally created the conflict,
- romantic subplot,
- hidden biological-family twist.

Do not lock speculative twists merely because they are possible.

Suggested macro structure:

## Phase 1 — Entry

Episodes approximately 1–10

Function:

Establish loss, infiltration, identity risk, first evidence.

## Phase 2 — Confirmation Pressure

Episodes approximately 11–20

Function:

Evidence appears to support protagonist belief.

## Phase 3 — Contradiction

Episodes approximately 21–30

Function:

Important evidence weakens or complicates the original theory.

## Phase 4 — Reorientation

Episodes approximately 31–40

Function:

Protagonist changes alliances and investigates deeper truth.

## Phase 5 — Resolution Pressure

Episodes approximately 41–50

Function:

Final responsibility, inheritance truth, identity exposure, and emotional choice converge.

These episode ranges are:

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE

not measured performance guidance.

# 28. Final Rule Demonstrated

A promising concept does not require every answer to be known by the protagonist.

But the production system should eventually know the difference between:

WHAT IS TRUE

WHAT A CHARACTER CLAIMS

WHAT EVIDENCE SHOWS

WHAT A CHARACTER KNOWS

WHAT A CHARACTER BELIEVES

and

WHAT THE AUDIENCE HAS BEEN ALLOWED TO SEE.

That separation is what turns a revenge premise into a controllable serialized story system.
