WHAT MUST BE REVEALED

for unresolved information.

Use:

for temporary working assumptions.

# 50. Final Review Questions

Before locking the Episode Contract, ask:

Does the episode materially change something?

Does it inherit the correct opening state?

Does the protagonist have an active objective?

Does conflict escalate?

Is required information introduced?

Is forbidden information still protected?

Does the episode pay or advance existing story debt?

Does the cliffhanger create a real next-episode obligation?

Are claims separated from facts?

Are observations separated from interpretations?

Are knowledge and belief separated?

Is temporal scope preserved?

Are physical and prop states coherent?

Are required state changes explicit?

Are production dependencies visible?

Are QC requirements defined?

---

# 51. Final Rule

A successful Episode Contract should make the episode inspectable before generation.

It should answer:

WHAT CHANGES?

WHY DOES IT CHANGE?

WHAT MUST BE REVEALED?

WHAT MUST REMAIN HIDDEN?

WHAT STORY PROMISE IS PAID?

WHAT NEW PROMISE IS CREATED?

WHAT STATE MUST PRODUCTION PRESERVE?

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS EPISODE FAIL?

The Episode Contract is not the finished episode.

It is the control layer that prevents the finished episode from drifting away from the series.
